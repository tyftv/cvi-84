CVI-84 Manual

CVI-84 recreates the Fairlight CVI, a 1984 video effects machine behind a lot of 80s music videos: trails, freezes, psychedelic colours, chunky pixels and mirror effects. You load a video, stack effects on it, play with them live, and save the result as an MP4.

Quick start

  1. Click Import video (top left) or drag a video onto the black screen. Any format works. If your browser can't play it directly (AVI, WMV and so on), it gets converted first, which takes a few seconds.
  2. On the left, click an effect, for example Colorize. It appears in the chain at the bottom of the screen and you see it on your video right away.
  3. Change how it looks with the controls on the right.
  4. Add more effects. They stack in order.
  5. Press Export MP4 (top right) to save the finished video.
Use Chrome or Edge on a computer. Other browsers may play the effects but can't always save the MP4.

The screen

  • Left: the library. The Effects tab lists every effect you can add. The Presets tab has the CVI's 100 factory setups and your saved chains.
  • Middle: the viewer. Your video with every effect applied. Above it is the paint toolbar. Below it are play, freeze and record.
  • Right: the settings. The controls for whichever effect you clicked in the chain. Click Output in the chain to see project settings instead.
  • Bottom: the chain. Your effects in order. The Automation tab next to it shows anything you've recorded.

How the chain works

Think of guitar pedals. The video goes into the first effect, comes out changed, goes into the next one, and so on until it reaches Output.

Video 1→Chroma key→Trail→Colorize→VHS→Output

Order matters. VHS then Colorize looks different from Colorize then VHS, because each effect only sees what the ones before it made. A good rule: effects that change the picture go first, and looks like VHS and CRT go at the end.

  • Add an effect: click it in the library. It's inserted right after the effect you have selected (or at the end).
  • Edit: click it in the chain. Its controls appear on the right.
  • Reorder: drag it left or right in the chain.
  • Turn off for a second: click its power icon. It stays in the chain but does nothing until you turn it back on.
  • Remove: click its ×, or select it and press Delete.
  • Clear chain (bottom right) removes everything.

You can add the same effect more than once. Each CVI effect in the chain is its own complete CVI machine, which is how people chained real CVIs together back in the day.

Every effect

Effects marked "keyed" need to know what your background is. See Key below.

Key
Chroma key
Removes a coloured background (green screen, blue screen, a plain wall) and puts something else behind you: black, a colour you pick, Video 2, or the canvas.Click Pick from video, then click your background. Put it first in the chain.
Luma key
Same idea, but keeps whatever is brighter (or darker) than a threshold instead of keying on a colour.Good for shots against a black or white background.
Color
Colorize
The classic CVI colour effects: rainbow false colour (Spectrum), Solarize, sepia, posterize, negatives and tints. Pick a Style, then move Hue and Intensity.Also turns your video into chunky CVI pixels, like the real machine.
Colour crawl
Keyed. Your subject leaves a trail whose colours ripple and crawl.
Pattern colourize
Colourizes the picture through a moving pattern (grid, hearts, checkerboard, stripes). Click a pattern to change it.
Shatter
Flickers a pattern between the normal picture and a colourized one. The Rate sets how fast.
Time
Trail
Keyed. The subject moves live and leaves frozen copies of itself behind, the signature CVI look.Turn on Scroll inside it to make the copies drift.
Colour trail
Keyed. Like Trail, but the subject leaves a splash of changing colour instead of copies.
Strobe
Grabs a frame and holds it, over and over, so motion looks choppy and stuttery. Rate sets how often.
Ghost
A held frame and the live video blended together, like a double exposure.
Catch-up
Keyed. The subject plays live while a delayed copy of the scene lags behind.
Feedback
Feeds the effect's own output back into itself, like pointing a camera at its own TV. Turn up Pixelate or Scroll for tunnels and smears.
Slow scan
Freezes the picture one column at a time, sweeping across, so anything moving gets stretched and warped. Press Run across or Run down.
Shape
Pixelate
Big chunky pixels. Pixelate sets the block size, Stretch makes them wide or tall.
Mirror
Mirrors the picture left/right, top/bottom, or both for a four-way kaleidoscope. Mirror line moves where the fold is.
Scroll
The picture slides sideways or up and down forever, wrapping around. Speed X and Y set direction and speed.
Paint
Paint layer
Draw, fill and write text over your video with the CVI paintbox. Pick a tool above the viewer and drag on the picture. See Painting and text.
Colour tunnel
Keyed. Rings of colour radiate out from the centre behind your subject. Press Start tunnel.
Video 2

These mix in a second video. Load it with + Video 2 at the top first.

Wipe to Video 2
A transition from your video to Video 2: sweep across, sweep down, or disintegrate.
Double exposure
Your video and Video 2 blended on top of each other.
Video 1/2 flip
Flickers back and forth between the two videos at the Rate.
Look

These don't do CVI tricks; they make the result look like old media. Put them at the end of the chain.

Black & white
Black and white film with grain, colour filters and toning (sepia, blue and so on).
Halftone
Printed newspaper or comic dots: black and white, full CMYK colour, or colour dots.
VHS
Videotape: smeared colour, tracking wobble, snow, dropouts and the noisy band at the bottom.
CRT
An old TV tube: curved glass, scanlines, the RGB phosphor pattern, glow and flicker.
Advanced
CVI unit
A whole CVI with every single setting showing. Presets load into one of these. Use it when you want to build something from scratch exactly like the original machine.

Settings you'll see a lot

On and Amount

Every effect has these at the top. On turns it off without removing it. Amount blends it with the picture underneath: 100% is full strength, 50% is half, 0% is nothing.

Key

Keyed effects split the picture into "subject" and "background." The original machine expected a blue backdrop, so it's set to blue by default. To use your own footage:

  1. Click Pick from video.
  2. Click on your background in the picture.
  3. If too much or too little disappears, adjust Tolerance.
  4. If it's keeping the wrong part, turn on Swap subject / background.

Plain, evenly lit backgrounds key best. A busy room won't key cleanly; that's how keying works on any machine.

Freeze & strobe

  • Live: updates every frame (normal).
  • Strobe: grabs a frame every so often, set by Rate.
  • Burst: grabs for a moment, holds for a moment, repeat.
  • Single: only grabs when you press Grab a frame or F.
  • Music: grabs on bass hits in your video's soundtrack.

The Freeze button under the viewer (or F) holds the selected effect's picture until you press it again.

Intensity

Intensity is centred: in the middle it does nothing, to the right is the normal effect, and to the left is the opposite (usually a negative version). That's how the original's Colour Depth slider worked.

Colour comes from

Sliders uses the Hue/Saturation/Brightness you set. Hold keeps the colour locked even if you turn Colorize off. Music makes bass red, mids green and treble blue. Random jumps to new colours on its own; Change speed sets how often.

Show all CVI settings

Each effect only shows the controls that matter for it, to keep things simple. The button at the bottom of the right panel reveals everything the real machine could do for that effect: routing, masks, paint, fills and so on.

Painting and text

Pick a tool in the bar above the viewer. If no CVI effect is selected, a Paint layer gets added for you.

Paint on: Picture / Mask / Both. Picture paints colour. Mask paints the invisible "stencil" that decides where effects show; for example, in a Paint layer, painting the mask cuts windows in the paint. Ctrl+Z undoes painting.

The Paint section on the right has the brush shapes, paint styles (spatter, mottled, rainbow), blend modes, symmetry (mirror painting and kaleidoscope), and textures. The Fill canvas section floods the whole thing with a colour, a sweep, a colour tunnel, colour bars and so on.

Presets and saved chains

The Presets tab has the CVI's original 100 factory presets with their real numbers and names. Click one and it loads into the selected effect, or gets added as a new CVI unit if nothing is selected. You can also press P then two digits (like 59 for Trail).

Under My chains, type a name and press Save chain to keep your whole chain with all its settings. Click it later to load it back. These are saved in your browser on this computer.

Recording changes over time

You can perform the effects live and have every move replayed in the export, like automation in a music program.

  1. Go back to the start (Home or the ⏮ button).
  2. Click Record so it lights up.
  3. Press play and move sliders, switch effects on and off, load presets, paint, press Freeze. Everything is written down against the video's time.
  4. Press pause when done. Open the Automation tab to see what got recorded.
  5. Play again (with Record off) and it all plays back. Export renders exactly that.

To redo one control, record over it: only the controls you actually touch get replaced. To delete a single recorded control, click the × next to it in the Automation tab. Clear automation removes everything.

A control with a small orange dot next to its name has a recording. While playing back, it moves on its own.

Exporting your video

  1. Click Export MP4.
  2. Pick the resolution and quality. "Same as source" keeps your original size.
  3. Click Render and wait. When it's done, the MP4 downloads.

The export always comes out at full quality, even if the preview stuttered, and keeps your original audio unless you turn it off. It uses the same shape as your video (portrait stays portrait).

Save project saves your chain, settings and recordings into a file so you can pick it back up later with Open project. It doesn't include the video itself, so import the same video again after opening.

Project settings

Click Output at the end of the chain.

  • Pixel detail: how big the CVI pixels are. Original is the real machine's resolution and the chunkiest. Fine and Extra fine are smaller, cleaner pixels but make your computer work harder.
  • Smooth pixels: softens the edges of the pixels instead of hard squares.
  • Hardware borders: adds the black edges the real CVI had around its picture.
  • Field clock: 50 Hz is the original (European) speed, 60 Hz is the American version. It changes how fast strobes, sweeps and rates run.

Recipes to try

80s music video trail

Chroma key (pick your background, Background: Black) → Trail → Colorize (Style: Spectrum) → CRT.

Haunted tape

Ghost (Freeze: Strobe, Rate around 40%) → Black & white (Toning up) → VHS (Tracking and Snow up).

Kaleidoscope

Mirror (both mirrors on) → Colorize (Style: Solarize) → Feedback (Pixelate a little) at Amount 50%.

Comic book

Colorize (Style: Posterize contours) → Halftone (Screen: CMYK).

Slow scan smear

Slow scan, press Run across while you move in front of the camera. Add VHS after it.

Something's wrong

The screen is black

That's normal until you import a video. If a video is loaded and it's still black, check the effects in the chain: turn them off one by one with the power icon to find the culprit. A Paint layer or Chroma key with nothing set up can cover the picture.

The key isn't working

Click Pick from video and click your background again, then raise Tolerance. If it keeps the wrong part, turn on Swap subject / background.

Playback is choppy

Lots of effects, or Extra fine pixels, are heavy. Try Original pixel detail or fewer effects while you work. The exported MP4 is never choppy.

My file won't load

Files the browser can't play get converted automatically. Very big files (over about 1.8 GB) are too large for that; convert them to MP4 first with HandBrake or Premiere.

Export won't start

Use Chrome or Edge. If it says the size isn't supported, pick 720p.

I want the old-school version

Click Classic console in the top bar for the original 1984-style console with its sliders, buttons and blue menu pages.
